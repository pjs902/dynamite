\section{Bayesian-optimization parameter search}
\label{app:bayesopt}

\subsection{Problem structure and notation}
\label{app:bayesopt:structure}
Triaxial Schwarzschild models separate into a cheap inner problem and an
expensive outer one \citep{vdbosch2019dynamite}. Given a trial potential
$\theta$, the orbit-library weights $w \ge 0$ that best reproduce the
observed kinematics follow from a bounded non-negative least-squares
solve,
\begin{equation}
  \chi^2(\theta) = \min_{w \ge 0} \|\A(\theta)\, w - b\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
which takes seconds; $\A(\theta)$ is the design matrix mapping orbit
histograms to observables and $b$ collects the data. The outer problem,
$\min_\theta \chi^2(\theta)$, requires one orbit-library integration per
trial potential, taking hours on a multicore node. Classical
implementations search $\theta$ with a grid walk \citep{zhu2018crcut}:
axis-aligned steps around the current best point, which costs
$O(2d)$ evaluations per accepted move in $d$ free parameters and can
stall when every neighbour is already evaluated.

Let the free parameters be $\theta \in \prod_j [\ell_j, h_j]$, with
logarithmic parameters (e.g.\ $M_\mathrm{BH}$, halo mass) entering
through their base-10 logarithms so that the search is always performed
on the raw value. We work with the normalized vector
\begin{equation}
  x_j = \frac{\theta_j - \ell_j}{h_j - \ell_j} \in [0,1]^d .
  \label{eq:norm}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Gaussian-process surrogate}
\label{app:bayesopt:gp}
After every evaluated batch, the surrogate is refit on all historical
rows $(x_i, y_i)$ with $y_i = -\chi^2_i$ (finite $\chi^2$ only). We use
a Gaussian process with a Mat\'ern~5/2 kernel with automatic relevance
determination,
\begin{equation}
  k(x, x') = \sigma^2 \prod_{j=1}^{d} \bigl(1 + \sqrt{5}\, r_j +
  \tfrac{5}{3} r_j^2\bigr) \exp\bigl(-\sqrt{5}\, r_j\bigr),
  \qquad r_j = \frac{|x_j - x'_j|}{\lambda_j},
\end{equation}
a separate length scale $\lambda_j$ per parameter, and outcome
standardization $(y_i - \bar y)/s_y$. The model is fit by empirical
Bayes (marginal-likelihood optimization, L-BFGS-B) in double precision.
Above 300 training rows the exact model is replaced by a variational GP
with a fixed inducing grid, since exact fitting scales as $O(n^3)$. The
surrogate is cheap relative to one model: on the benchmark suite the
full generate step (fit plus acquisition optimization) averages
$0.15\,$s per proposed model, against hours per integrated model in
production.

\subsection{Acquisition and batch selection}
\label{app:bayesopt:acq}
The next batch $X_{1:q} = \{x_1, \dots, x_q\}$ is chosen to jointly
maximize the logarithm of the $q$-point Expected Improvement
\citep[qLogEI;][]{eriksson2019turbo},
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{qLogEI}(X_{1:q}) =
  \log \mathbb{E}\!\left[\max\bigl(0,\; y^\star -
  \max(X_{1:q})\bigr)\right],
\end{equation}
where the expectation is over the GP posterior and $y^\star$ is the best
observed value. The inner expectation is taken in the log space for
numerical stability at large $\chi^2$ scales. The joint problem is
optimized with multi-restart L-BFGS-B (10 restarts, 128 raw starting
points); with active constraints, starting points are drawn from the
feasible set and the batch is optimized one element at a time
(\texttt{batch\_limit}=1).

Three modifications to plain qLogEI matter in practice.

\paragraph{(a) Annealed exploration.} The indicator temperature $\eta$
in qLogEI's smooth improvement heuristic decays linearly over the first
$N_\mathrm{ann}=10$ batches,
\begin{equation}
  \eta(t) = \eta_0 + \tfrac{t}{N_\mathrm{ann}}\,(\eta_\infty - \eta_0),
  \qquad \eta_0 = 0.1,\; \eta_\infty = 10^{-3},
\end{equation}
so early batches soften the improvement indicator (exploration) and
later batches sharpen it (exploitation). This is the dimension-scaled
exploration schedule of \citet{elgammal2023gpry}, adapted from their
$\zeta = d^{-0.85}$ relaxation.

\paragraph{(b) Tempered-posterior batch members.} A fraction
$n_\mathrm{R2} = \lceil q/4 \rceil$ of each batch is drawn not from the
acquisition optimizer but by rejection sampling from the annealed
surrogate posterior,
\begin{equation}
  p_t(x) \propto \exp\!\left(\frac{\mu_t(x) - \max_x \mu_t}{\tau_t}\right)
  \quad \text{on the feasible set},
  \qquad \tau_t = \max(\tau_\mathrm{min},\, \tau_0\,\gamma^{\,t}),
\end{equation}
with $\mu_t$ the posterior mean, $\tau_0 = 1$, $\gamma = 0.7$,
$\tau_\mathrm{min} = 0.05$. Candidates are drawn in Sobol chunks of
$\max(256, 16\,n_\mathrm{R2})$ and accepted with probability
$\exp((\mu - \mu_\mathrm{max})/\tau)$; if fewer than $n_\mathrm{R2}$
accept within $2\times10^5$ draws, the remainder falls back to feasible
quasi-random points. This is the annealed-objective mechanism of
\citet{li2026sale}, simplified to the posterior mean, and it keeps
batches diverse where joint acquisition optimization homogenizes them.

\paragraph{(c) Grid-cell deduplication for orbit-library reuse.}
Orbit integration depends on all parameters except $M_\mathrm{L}$, which
enters only through the scaling of the weight solve. When the
non-$M_\mathrm{L}$ parameters are discretized on their generator grids
(steps $s_j$), candidates are snapped, $x_j \mapsto s_j
\lfloor x_j / s_j \rceil$, and keyed by the tuple
$\kappa(x) = (\lfloor x_j / s_j \rceil)_{j \in \text{non-}M_\mathrm{L}}$.
Duplicates within a batch (same key) are dropped, first-proposed kept,
and freed slots refilled with feasible Sobol draws. Candidates sharing a
key but differing in $M_\mathrm{L}$ are retained: they reuse one orbit
library across several weight solves at no integration cost, which the
batch layout ($n_\mathrm{orb}$ potential configurations $\times$
$n_\mathrm{ml}$ mass values each) exploits deliberately.

\subsection{Triaxial feasibility}
\label{app:bayesopt:feas}
With intrinsic flattenings $q \le p \le u$ (where $u =
\sigma_\mathrm{obs}/\sigma_\mathrm{int}$) and observed flattening
$q_\mathrm{obs}$, a parameter set is dynamically consistent iff
\begin{equation}
  p \ge q, \qquad
  u \ge \max\!\left(\frac{q}{q_\mathrm{obs}},\, p\right), \qquad
  u \le \min\!\left(\frac{p}{q_\mathrm{obs}},\, 1\right).
  \label{eq:feas}
\end{equation}
When all three axes are free, Eq.~\eqref{eq:feas} is imposed on the
acquisition optimizer as differentiable inequality constraints
($p - q \ge 0$; $u - \max(q/q_\mathrm{obs}, p) \ge 0$;
$\min(p/q_\mathrm{obs}, 1) - u \ge 0$), evaluated on the un-normalized
coordinates inside the constraint callables. When a subset is free, the
fixed axes enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:feas} as constants. For the common
production case $(q, p)$ free with $u = u_f$ fixed, the window
constraints collapse to bounds,
\begin{equation}
  q \le u_f\, q_\mathrm{obs}, \qquad
  u_f\, q_\mathrm{obs} \le p \le u_f, \qquad p \ge q ,
  \label{eq:uproj}
\end{equation}
and Sobol warm-up draws are projected onto
Eq.~\eqref{eq:uproj} with a relative safety margin of $10^{-6}$
($q \le u_f q_\mathrm{obs} (1 - 10^{-6})$, etc.). The same inequalities
guard GP-phase proposals: any candidate violating them after
optimization is replaced by the nearest feasible point of the same
batch. A fuzz suite asserts these invariants over randomized free
subsets, including logarithmic axes, with zero violations in 455 active
trials.

\subsection{Warm-up, warm start, and stopping}
\label{app:bayesopt:warm}
The first $n_\mathrm{init}$ candidates come from a scrambled Sobol
sequence (projected feasible). Optionally an axial design is used
instead: the center point $x^{(c)}$ plus $2d$ axis steps of size
$\delta = 0.1$, i.e.\ $2d + 1$ points. When the generator is started in
an output directory with existing evaluations, for example after a grid
walk, the Sobol phase is skipped entirely ($n_\mathrm{valid} \ge
n_\mathrm{init}$) and the GP trains on the full history; rows outside
the current bounds are clipped to them and counted per axis in a
warning. With the axial design and no explicit center, the center
defaults to the best historical row.

Alongside the inherited $\chi^2$-plateau criterion, two diagnostics are
computed. The prediction-accuracy counter \citep[CorrectCounter;
][]{elgammal2023gpry} scores each finished model by
\begin{equation}
  |\mu(x_i) - y_i| \;\le\; \epsilon_\mathrm{abs} +
  \epsilon_\mathrm{rel}\,\bigl|y^\star - \mu(x_i)\bigr|,
  \qquad \epsilon_\mathrm{rel} = 0.01,
\end{equation}
where $\mu(x_i)$ was recorded at proposal time; a streak of
$m = \max(4, \lceil d/2 \rceil)$ accurate predictions raises the
\texttt{gp\_predictions\_accurate} flag. This is reported but never
stops the search on its own; at $\epsilon_\mathrm{rel}=0.01$ the flag
did not fire in 50 benchmark runs, indicating the tolerance is
conservative for these landscapes. Optionally, a single trust region
\citep[TuRBO-style;][]{eriksson2019turbo} restricts acquisition to the
box $x^\star \pm s/2$ around the incumbent once the mean distance from
the incumbent to its five nearest evaluated neighbours falls below
$0.1/\sqrt{d}$; the side $s$ starts at $0.3$, multiplies by $1.3$ on any
batch that improves the incumbent, and by $0.7$ after two consecutive
unimproving batches, clipped to $[0.05, 0.6]$.

\subsection{Validation and tuning}
\label{app:bayesopt:valid}
We validate on three levels. (a) Unit and property tests cover the
feasibility projection for all free/non-free subsets of $(q,p,u)$, the
warm-start contract against a real run table, batch deduplication, and a
fuzz suite over randomized parameter spaces. (b) Synthetic $\chi^2$
surfaces, calibrated to the parameter ranges of our galaxies, encode the
structures that make these searches hard: a mass--flattening valley, a
black-hole bowl over a correlated shape band, and a curved
constant-mass degeneracy valley with a secondary basin. On the coupled
four-parameter surface, Bayesian optimization reaches the convergence
threshold in a median of 42 fresh models against 116 for the grid walk
over six seeds, with a better final $\chi^2$ ($6013$ versus $6070$); on
single-valley surfaces the grid walk remains competitive, which bounds
the recommendation. A hard acceptance test requires the generator to
reach the threshold in fewer models than the grid walk and keeps
surrogate overhead below ten seconds per model. (c) On NGC~6278 with
three free parameters and equal model budgets, the generator reaches
$\chi^2 = 5814$ where the grid walk reaches $6352$, and finds its
optimum between grid points.

A hyperparameter study on the coupled surface (five seeds per
configuration) found the batch size the dominant setting: $q = 4$
reaches the threshold in a median of 26 fresh models against 41 for
$q = 8$ and 56 for $q = 16$, consistent with the guideline of
\citet{elgammal2023gpry} that the batch should be of order
$\min(d, n_\mathrm{workers})$. Warm-up size, the $\eta$ schedule, and
the tempered-posterior dose were neutral at $d = 4$; removing trust
regions cost 46 versus 41. Seeding the GP from sparse uniformly
distributed history at $d = 4$ degrades the search relative to a cold
start, because uniform draws under-resolve the black-hole bowl;
warm-starting from a clustered, near-optimal history (the situation a
finished grid walk leaves) is the supported production path.
